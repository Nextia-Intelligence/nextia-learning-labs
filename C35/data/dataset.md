## What one row means

Larkfield is a made-up online shop for home and garden products, with a help desk. The history runs from Monday 1 April 2024 to Sunday 29 March 2026 (104 weeks).

| File | One row | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| `demand.csv.gz` | one product in one region on one day: `price`, `on_promotion` (1 or 0), units `sales`, and `stock_out` (1 if the shelf was empty for part or all of the day) | 403,758 |
| `orders.csv` | one sales channel (web, app, phone) on one day: the number of orders | 2,181 |
| `tickets_daily.csv` | one help-desk channel (email, chat, phone) on one day | 2,184 |
| `tickets_hourly.csv.gz` | one help-desk channel in one hour (`timestamp` is the start of the hour) | 52,416 |
| `roster_hourly.csv` | one hour: the number of agents that were scheduled | 17,472 |
| `weather.csv` | one region on one day: the mean temperature in °C | 2,184 |
| `calendar.csv` | one day, to 28 June 2026: the weekday and the holiday | 819 |
| `products.csv` | one product: name, category, base price, first day on sale | 200 |
| `promotions.csv` | one planned promotion week, to 28 June 2026: product, first and last day, discount | 410 |
| `price_changes.csv` | one permanent price change, announced in advance | 3 |

There are 200 products in 10 categories, sold in 3 regions (north, south, coast): 600 series of one product in one region.

## Why this dataset

Forecasting needs data with the problems that real shops have, and with a future that we can reveal later. This data has a weekly and a yearly season, a trend, moving and fixed holidays (Easter, Black Friday, Christmas), planned promotions and price changes, the weather for garden products, slow sellers with many days of zero sales, new products with little history, stock-outs, and a missing day. A real shop's data would not be free to share, and its future would already be known.

## Changes we made

None after generation. The 13 weeks after the history (30 March to 28 June 2026) are kept back by the course team: they score the final assignment, as reality would.

## Limitations and cautions

- **The data is synthetic.** It has no real people, products, prices or places. Do not conclude anything about a real shop from it.
- **Sales are not demand.** On a `stock_out` day, customers wanted more than the shelf had. The real demand is not in the data.
- **One day is missing:** 2025-10-19 has no rows in `demand.csv.gz` and `orders.csv` (the feed dropped it). The ticket files are complete.
- **Situations we planted** (the lessons name them): a cold summer in 2025 with one heat-wave week, a warm early spring in 2025, a promotion that doubles sales, a price rise that moves the level for good, a nine-day stock-out, a slow seller that sells about once a month, a product with six weeks of history.
- Two years hold only two examples of every yearly pattern. Read a yearly season with care.
