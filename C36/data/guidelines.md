# Larkfield labelling guidelines: which route for a message?

Chloé wrote these guidelines for the people who label Larkfield's support messages. Each message gets **one route**: the team that must act first, or `needs_person`. The messages are synthetic: we made them for the course.

## The one rule before all others

**Label the request, not the words.** A message that says "refund" is not always a refund request. Ask: *what does the customer want Larkfield to do?* If the customer asks for two things, label the **main request**: usually the first one, or the one that is urgent.

## The routes

| Route | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `orders` | A change to an order **before it ships**: change the address, the item or the quantity, cancel it, or a missing order confirmation. | "Please cancel order LK-123456, I ordered it twice." |
| `delivery` | Where a parcel is: late, lost, not moved in tracking, a new delivery day, or **marked delivered but not received**. Delivery costs and areas. | "Tracking says delivered yesterday, but nothing is at my door." |
| `returns_refunds` | A return or an exchange, an item that **arrived damaged or wrong**, and the status of a refund **after a return**. | "I sent the chair back two weeks ago. When do I get my money?" |
| `billing` | Charges and payments: a **double or wrong charge**, a failed payment, an invoice or a receipt, a promotion code or a gift card. | "There are two charges of $89.99 on my card for one order." |
| `product` | A question about a product, **before or after** buying: size, materials, stock, assembly, care, warranty. Questions about the return policy **before** buying. | "Can the bench stay outside in winter?" |
| `account` | The customer's Larkfield account: login, password, personal details, deleting the account, stopping emails. **Account takeover**: someone else changed the account or placed an order. | "Someone changed my email and placed an order I did not make." |
| `needs_person` | A person must read it first: a **safety problem** (injury, smoke, fire, a child at risk), a **legal or press threat** (lawyer, consumer protection office, journalist), or a **complaint that asks for a manager** after several tries. | "The heater started smoking when I turned it on." |

## Hard cases between two routes

| Between | Choose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `returns_refunds` and `billing` | **Refund after a return** → `returns_refunds`. **A charge that is wrong** (twice, too much) → `billing`. | The returns team tracks returned items; the billing team fixes charges. Two careful people disagree here most often: read the whole message. |
| `returns_refunds` and `delivery` | **Arrived damaged or wrong** → `returns_refunds`. **Did not arrive** → `delivery`. | The returns team sends a replacement; the delivery team talks to the courier. |
| `returns_refunds` and `product` | "Can I return it if I don't like it?" **before buying** → `product`. **After** buying, the customer wants to return it → `returns_refunds`. | A question about the policy is a product question. A keyword rule on "return" gets this wrong. |
| `product` and `returns_refunds` | **Broke after some weeks or months of use** (warranty) → `product`. **Broken on arrival** → `returns_refunds`. | |
| `account` and `billing` | An order the customer did not make **and** a changed login or email → `account` (account takeover). A charge the customer does not understand, with no sign of someone else in the account → `billing`. | An account takeover is urgent: the account team locks the account first. |
| any team and `needs_person` or `account` | **The route follows the main request.** When the main request is about safety, a legal or press threat or a manager → `needs_person`; when it is that someone else used the account → `account`. When one of these is **only mentioned in passing** ("still no parcel; also, my son got hurt"), keep the main request's route, **raise the priority** and **flag** the message for a person. | Each team owns the requests it can solve, and every label keeps one meaning. The flag is part of the rule: a team must not answer an injury or a legal threat without a person. |
| `orders` and `delivery` | The order **has not shipped** and the customer wants a change → `orders`. It **has shipped** → `delivery`. | |

## Messages that are not clear

- **Vague messages** ("see my last email", "still nothing"): choose the route that the facts point to (an order number with "still nothing" usually means `delivery`). If you cannot tell, label your best guess and mark the message for review; do not use another route as a "not sure" label.
- **Sarcasm** ("Great, another week without my table. Love it."): label what the customer wants, not the words.
- **French messages** follow the same rules. Product names stay in English: they are Larkfield's catalogue names.

## Priority

`urgent`: an account takeover, a safety problem, a legal threat, a double charge of a large amount, a parcel marked delivered but not received, or a customer who gives a deadline in the next day or two. `low`: an invoice, a promotion code, a question about stock or a product with no deadline. Everything else is `normal`. A safety problem, a legal threat or a takeover that is only mentioned in passing raises the priority, and the message gets a flag (see the hard cases).
