# Judging guidelines for Larkfield's product search

You see one query and one product. Decide how well the product answers the query, **for a person who typed this
query in Larkfield's search box**. Judge the product, not the photo or the price. Use the title, the product
class, the description and the attributes.

| Grade | Name | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | **Exact** | The product is what the person asked for, with every detail in the query (type, colour, size, material, brand or name). |
| 1 | **Partial** | The product is the right kind of thing, but one detail is different or missing; or it is a close substitute. |
| 0 | **Irrelevant** | The product is not what the person asked for (another kind of product, an accessory for it, a part of it). |

Rules for hard cases:

1. **Accessories are irrelevant.** "sofa" → a sofa cover or a sofa table is 0, even though the word matches.
2. **A missing detail makes it partial.** "grey outdoor cushion" → a blue outdoor cushion is 1.
3. **A different size is partial**, unless the size is the whole point of the query ("48 inch vanity" → a 30 inch vanity is 1).
4. **Brand or name queries:** "kisner coffee table" → the Kisner coffee table is 2; another coffee table is 1; a Kisner sofa is 0.
5. **If you cannot tell,** read the description and the attributes. If you still cannot tell, choose the lower grade and write a note.
6. **Language does not matter:** a French query and an English product can be an exact match.

These are the grades of WANDS (exact, partial, irrelevant). The WANDS annotators followed their own guidelines;
ours are written to match them, so your grades can be compared with theirs.
