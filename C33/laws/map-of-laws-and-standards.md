# Map of laws, standards and frameworks

**This is not legal advice.** It explains in plain words what each text is for, who it applies to and what it asks, so that you know what to ask a lawyer. Laws in this area change often: each entry says when its official text was checked. Check the current text before you rely on it.

## Four kinds of rule

| Kind | Binding? | Examples below |
|---|---|---|
| **Law** | Yes, for the people it applies to | EU AI Act, GDPR, Digital Omnibus on AI, PIPEDA, Quebec private-sector privacy act |
| **Bill** | No: a proposed law that may change or never pass | Bill C-27 (AIDA), Bill C-34, Bill C-36 |
| **Government policy** | Yes, but only for the government bodies it names | Directive on Automated Decision-Making, UK ATRS |
| **Standard** | Only if you choose it, or a contract or a law asks for it; some can be certified | ISO/IEC 23894, ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 42005 |
| **Framework** | No: voluntary guidance | NIST crosswalk to ISO/IEC 42005, NIST generative AI profile, NIST AI RMF, UK approach to AI regulation |
| **Contract** | Yes, between the parties who sign it | your vendors' terms; your customers' contracts |

## The entries

### EU AI Act

| | |
|---|---|
| Official source | [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the EU AI Act)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) |
| Kind | law |
| Where | European Union |
| What it is for | Sorts AI uses by risk: banned practices, high-risk systems, transparency duties for some systems, and rules for general-purpose AI models. Gives duties to providers and to deployers. |
| Who it applies to | Providers and deployers of AI systems in the EU, and companies outside the EU whose AI output is used in the EU. |
| Checked | 22 October 2026 |

### Digital Omnibus on AI

| | |
|---|---|
| Official source | [Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital Omnibus on AI, which amends the AI Act)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj) |
| Kind | law |
| Where | European Union |
| What it is for | Moves the start of the high-risk rules later, rewrites the AI literacy duty, adds two banned practices and gives relief to smaller companies. |
| Who it applies to | The same people as the AI Act. |
| Checked | 22 October 2026 |

### GDPR

| | |
|---|---|
| Official source | [Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (the GDPR), Article 22](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) |
| Kind | law |
| Where | European Union |
| What it is for | Protects personal data. Article 22 gives a person the right not to be subject to a decision based only on automated processing that has legal or similarly significant effects, with exceptions. |
| Who it applies to | Organisations that process personal data of people in the EU. |
| Checked | 22 October 2026 |

### NIST AI RMF

| | |
|---|---|
| Official source | [NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, NIST AI 100-1)](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) |
| Kind | framework |
| Where | United States (used everywhere) |
| What it is for | A voluntary framework with four functions: govern, map, measure, manage. NIST says a revision is in progress. |
| Who it applies to | Anyone who chooses to use it. |
| Checked | 22 October 2026 |

### NIST generative AI profile

| | |
|---|---|
| Official source | [NIST AI 600-1, Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf) |
| Kind | framework |
| Where | United States (used everywhere) |
| What it is for | A companion to the AI RMF for generative AI. It lists 12 risks, for example confabulation, data privacy and harmful bias. |
| Who it applies to | Anyone who chooses to use it. |
| Checked | 22 October 2026 |

### NIST crosswalk to ISO/IEC 42005

| | |
|---|---|
| Official source | [NIST AI Resource Center: crosswalk from ISO/IEC 42005 to the AI RMF](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/crosswalks/) |
| Kind | framework |
| Where | United States |
| What it is for | Maps the AI RMF functions to the parts of the impact-assessment standard. NIST says that a crosswalk is not an endorsement. |

### Bill C-27 (AIDA)

| | |
|---|---|
| Official source | [Bill C-27 (with the proposed Artificial Intelligence and Data Act), 44th Parliament](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/44-1/c-27) |
| Kind | bill |
| Where | Canada (federal) |
| What it is for | Would have created a federal AI law. It did not become law: the session of Parliament ended while it was still in committee. |
| Who it applies to | Nobody: it is not law. |
| Checked | 22 October 2026 |

### PIPEDA

| | |
|---|---|
| Official source | [Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA), S.C. 2000, c. 5](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/page-7.html) |
| Kind | law |
| Where | Canada (federal) |
| What it is for | Canada's federal privacy law for private-sector organisations. An organisation is responsible for the personal information it controls, also when a vendor processes it, and names a person accountable. |
| Who it applies to | Private-sector organisations in Canada (where no similar provincial law applies instead). |
| Checked | 22 October 2026 |

### Bill C-36

| | |
|---|---|
| Official source | [Bill C-36 (Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act), 45th Parliament](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-36) |
| Kind | bill |
| Where | Canada (federal) |
| What it is for | A privacy bill. As written, it would give people a right to an explanation of a decision made with an automated decision system. It is not law and may change. |
| Who it applies to | Nobody yet: it is a bill. |
| Checked | 22 October 2026 |

### Quebec private-sector privacy act

| | |
|---|---|
| Official source | [Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector (Quebec), CQLR c P-39.1, section 12.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/p-39.1) |
| Kind | law |
| Where | Quebec |
| What it is for | A business that makes a decision based only on automated processing of personal information must tell the person, explain the information and the main reasons on request, and let the person submit observations to a staff member who can review the decision. |
| Who it applies to | Businesses that hold personal information about people in Quebec. |
| Checked | 22 October 2026 |

### Directive on Automated Decision-Making

| | |
|---|---|
| Official source | [Directive on Automated Decision-Making (Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat)](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32592) |
| Kind | policy |
| Where | Canada (federal government) |
| What it is for | Federal institutions must complete and publish an Algorithmic Impact Assessment before they use an automated decision system. It sets an impact level from I to IV. |
| Who it applies to | Federal government institutions only, not private companies. |
| Checked | 22 October 2026 |

## Paid standards

ISO/IEC standards are sold by ISO and national standards bodies. This course explains them from public summaries and its own templates, and does not copy their text.

## Keep a rule watch

1. One person owns this map (Farida at Larkfield) and checks each entry at a fixed rhythm, for example every three months, and when the news reports a change.
2. For each change: does it touch one of our systems? Use the inventory and the tiers.
3. Write the questions for a lawyer before the meeting: the system, the data, the countries, what the output changes for a person.
4. Update this map, with the new date checked, and tell the system owners.
