# Case study: learn from a public algorithm failure

**The case:** in summer 2020, exams in England were cancelled. Ofqual, the exams regulator, used a statistical model to adjust the grades that teachers had proposed. Many students got lower grades than their teachers proposed, public confidence fell, and four days after the results the government switched back to the teachers' grades. The [source card](source-card.md) lists the official documents, their licence and the facts this course checked.

The model was a statistical method, not a large language model. That does not matter for this case study: the questions an impact assessment asks are the same for any system that decides something important about people.

## Your role

You are Farida. The governance board asks for training material: "Write the impact assessment that should have been done before these grades were issued. What would it have found?"

## What you hand in

The one-page [impact assessment](../../templates/impact-assessment.md) for the grading model, as if written before results day, with:

1. The people affected and how: students, schools and colleges, universities and employers who use grades. Which groups were more exposed?
2. The benefits and the harms, with the evidence that existed before results day. The interim report was published on results day (13 August 2020), but its results tables use data from "around a week before results were issued", and its tests of the model were done on 2019 students. Give the error for one student, not only the averages.
3. Fairness, rights and redress: how a student could find out why, appeal and get a correct grade, and how fast.
4. The controls, and two stop lines written before the results (one on people, one on groups), checked against the data that existed a week before results day.
5. What the assessment cannot know without testing on real results.

## Make it a portfolio piece

Turn it into a short memo: "Before we issue grades by model" (one page), with the impact assessment as an appendix. Cite the official documents, keep quotes short, and include the licence statement from the source card.
