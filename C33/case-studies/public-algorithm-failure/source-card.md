## Facts from the documents

| Fact | From the official text |
|---|---|
| What schools sent | Teachers gave each student a centre assessment grade and a rank order: "over 5 million CAGs and rank order positions" (executive summary). |
| Why the grades were adjusted | Ofqual found the teachers' grades "in general, optimistic". At A level, the share of A* grades would have risen from 7.7% in 2019 to 13.9% (executive summary). |
| What the model did | The "Direct Centre Performance model (DCP)" predicted a distribution of grades for each school or college from "the historical performance of the school or college in that subject". For groups of fewer than 15 students, it gave more weight to the teachers' grades (executive summary). |
| How many A level grades changed (England) | Unchanged 58.7%; one grade lower 35.6%; two grades lower 3.3%; three or more lower 0.2%; one grade higher 2.2% (Table 9.8, interim report, p. 135). So about 39% were lowered (our sum). |
| How many grades were issued at A* | 8.9% of A level grades, against 13.9% in the teachers' grades (Table 9.6, interim report, p. 134). |
| How accurate the model was for one student | The model was tested on 2019 students. Its key measure was "the probability of the predicted grade matching the grade actually achieved by the student" (interim report, p. 49). For the chosen model, that share was between 26.7% and 67.9%, by A level subject; within one grade, between 64.0% and 98.7% (Table E.8, interim report, pp. 204-205, 55 subjects). |
| Groups of students | "no evidence that this year's process of awarding grades has introduced bias" (executive summary, p. 5). By socio-economic status: "The levels of adjustment are similar across groups" (Annex Q, p. 1). |
| Types of school | Change at grade A and above, 2019 to 2020, in percentage points: Secondary comprehensive +2.0; Secondary selective +1.2; Independent +4.7; Sixth form/FE/tertiary +0.3; Academy +1.7; Other +3.6 (Table 9.10, interim report, p. 136; national change 2.4, executive summary, p. 4). The report: "This reflects differences in a typical year." |
| When the data existed | The report came out on results day (13 August 2020), but its results tables use data submitted "around a week before results were issued" (interim report, p. 136). |
| Appeals | Ofqual's chair: "Expecting schools to submit appeals where grades were incorrect placed a burden on teachers". The Education Secretary (Department for Education): "more inconsistency and unfairness than can be reasonably resolved through an appeals process". |
| The reversal | Ofqual's chair: "The switch to centre assessment grades will apply to both AS and A levels and to the GCSE results which students will receive later this week." The Department for Education said "the algorithm has revealed a number of anomalies that had not been anticipated by Ofqual and which severely undermined confidence in the system". |
