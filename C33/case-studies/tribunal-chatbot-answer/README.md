# Case study: a tribunal case about a chatbot's answer

**The decision:** *Moffatt v. Air Canada*, 2024 BCCRT 149, a decision of the Civil Resolution Tribunal of British Columbia. Read it on CanLII: <https://www.canlii.org/en/bc/bccrt/doc/2024/2024bccrt149/2024bccrt149.html>. The [source card](source-card.md) says what the decision is, how you may use it, and what this course checked.

**This is not legal advice**, and it is not a judgement of the company: you use a public decision to practise a governance review.

## Your role

You are Farida, Larkfield's risk and compliance lead. Nadia read about the case and asks: "Larkfield's support assistant answers customers too. Could this happen to us? What would have prevented it?"

## What you hand in

A governance review of two pages, in the [worksheet](worksheet.md):

1. **What happened**, in five sentences, from the decision itself, with paragraph numbers.
2. **What the tribunal decided and why**, in plain words.
3. **The governance gaps**: the policy, the owner, the review and the record that were missing or did not work. Use the course's tools: the inventory row, the tier, the responsibility table, the control map.
4. **What would have prevented it**: three controls, each with an owner and the evidence that it works.
5. **Larkfield**: which of Larkfield's systems could give a customer a wrong promise, and what Larkfield should change.

## Make it a portfolio piece

Write the review as a one-page memo to an executive: the case in three lines, the three controls, and the one decision you ask for. Cite the decision with its paragraph numbers. Say clearly that it is a governance exercise on a public decision, not legal advice.
