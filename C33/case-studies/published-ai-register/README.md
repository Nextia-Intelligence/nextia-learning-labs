# Case study: review a published AI register

**The records:** the Government of Canada publishes the Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) of each automated decision system that a federal institution uses. The Treasury Board's Directive on Automated Decision-Making requires it (see the map of laws and standards). `records.csv` has one row per published record, with its impact level from I (little impact) to IV (very high impact). The [source card](source-card.md) has the licence and how the file was made.

## Your role

You are Farida. Larkfield is a private company, so the Directive does not apply to it. But Nadia asks: "The government publishes its register. What can we learn from it for our own inventory, and what does a published register leave out?"

## What you hand in

1. **An inventory** of ten records of your choice in the course's [inventory](../../templates/inventory.md) columns. Which columns can you fill from the published record, and which not?
2. **Your own tier** for each of the ten with the course's [risk-tier method](../../templates/risk-tier-table.md). Compare it with the published impact level. Where do they differ, and why?
3. **What is missing**: what would you need to know before you trust a published impact level? Look at the spread of levels in the whole file.
4. **One recommendation** for Larkfield's own inventory.

## Make it a portfolio piece

A two-page review: the ten-row inventory, a short table "published level vs our tier", three findings and the recommendation. Include the licence statement from the source card.
