# Building and buying AI: stage gates in proportion to the risk

> A template for Larkfield's policy. The gates follow the leadership course's stage gates ([Leading AI Products and Teams](https://learning.nextia-ai.com/courses/ai-leadership/m03/milestones-and-roadmaps/)); here they are set by the tier of the [risk-tier table](risk-tier-table.md).

## Gates for a system Larkfield builds

| Gate | Low tier | Medium tier | High tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Idea: owner and tier | Owner named, inventory row, the two questions before scoring answered, tier set | Same | Same |
| 2. Before real data | Acceptable-use rules | A system card; privacy check if personal data | An impact assessment (draft); privacy or legal review |
| 3. Before users | No extra check | Evaluation result in a decision record; approval of actions designed; if it affects customers, an impact assessment that the owner signs | The board reviews the impact assessment; a release gate with written thresholds, passed |
| Who decides at gate 3 | The owner and the risk lead, within 2 working days | The accountable owner, after the checks above | The board |
| 4. After launch | Review in 12 months | Change log; review in 6 months | Monitoring with pause conditions; incident plan; review in 3 months |

Each tier also does what the lower tiers do, except what it replaces (for example, the high tier's review every 3 months replaces the review every 6 months).

## Review of an AI feature inside a bought tool

A bought tool can add an AI feature in an update. Treat that as a new system.

1. **Find it:** the contract owner, IT or the team that uses the tool tells the risk lead. Switched on by default is not "approved".
2. **Inventory row and tier**, as for a built system.
3. **Vendor check** with the [vendor questionnaire](vendor-questionnaire.md): for medium and high tier before it is switched on; for low tier within 30 days. If it is already on, the check is overdue: the owner decides at once whether it stays on until the check is done.
4. **Decision:** switch on, switch on with conditions, or keep off. Record it in a [decision record](decision-record.md).

## Test the policy with three "may I...?" questions

Write three questions that a team would really ask, and check that the policy alone answers each one. For example: "May we switch on the new AI feature of a tool we already pay for?"
