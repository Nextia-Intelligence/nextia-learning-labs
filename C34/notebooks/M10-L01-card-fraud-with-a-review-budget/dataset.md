## What one row means

One card payment. `ts` is the date and time; `card_id` the card (K-0001 to K-0999); `category` the kind of shop (14 categories such as `grocery_pos` or `shopping_net`; `_pos` in a shop, `_net` online); `amount` in US dollars; `is_fraud` 1 for a fraudulent payment, 0 for an honest one (9,651 frauds, 0.52%). `gender` and `birth_year` are simulated attributes of the cardholder, kept only to check whether the alerts treat groups differently; they are not model inputs.

## Why this dataset

The case study needs many payments per day, few frauds, a card ID (to compare a payment with the card's own past) and two years in time order (to train on the past and test on the future). The well-known ULB credit-card fraud data (Kaggle `mlg-ulb/creditcardfraud`) is licensed ODbL + DbCL, which this course does not use; it also covers only two days and has no card ID. PaySim (CC BY-SA), BankSim (CC BY-NC-SA) and the IEEE-CIS competition data (competition rules) have licences that this course does not use. IBM TabFormer (Apache-2.0) is similar but has 24 million rows. The full comparison is in the course's data research notes.

## Changes we made

- Joined `fraudTrain.csv` and `fraudTest.csv` (they are consecutive in time) and sorted by time.
- Replaced the card number `cc_num` by `card_id` K-0001 … K-0999, numbered in the order of each card's first payment.
- Removed the names, street, city, state, zip, job, transaction number, Unix time, merchant name and all coordinates. They are generated by the simulator, but they look like personal data, and the case study does not need them.
- Reduced `dob` to `birth_year`; renamed `trans_date_trans_time` to `ts`, `amt` to `amount`. Values are unchanged.
- Built by `reference/c34/m10/l01/make_sample.py` in the nextia-learning repository; the output is byte-identical on every run.

## Limitations and cautions

- **Simulated, not real.** No real cardholder or bank is behind these rows. Do not conclude how much fraud real banks have, or how well a method works on real payments.
- **Simulator patterns.** Almost every card (976 of 999) has exactly one fraud episode of about 10 payments in about 2 days; 84.6% of the frauds are between 22:00 and 03:59; every card that first appears after January 2019 (86 cards) starts with a fraud episode. Real fraud is less regular, and a detector that learns these patterns may learn the simulator.
- **December** has about twice as many honest payments as other months, and fewer frauds.
- `gender` and `birth_year` come from the simulator's customer profiles. Use them only for slice checks, never as inputs to a decision about a person.
