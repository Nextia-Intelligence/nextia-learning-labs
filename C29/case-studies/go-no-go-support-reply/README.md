# Case study 1: Go/no-go memo for a support reply assistant

*About 2 hours. No code. You need the [pilot evidence pack](../../evidence/pilot-evidence-pack/README.md), [Omar's proposal](proposal.md), the [evidence card](evidence-card.md) and the [go/no-go memo](../../templates/go-no-go-memo.md) template.*

## The situation

Larkfield's support reply assistant has run as a pilot: it drafts replies, and agents edit and send them. Omar, the product manager, now proposes three changes at once (read [his proposal](proposal.md)). Nadia, Larkfield's new head of customer operations, must decide on Friday. She asks you to review the proposal and the evidence, and to write her the memo.

## What you do

1. **Read the proposal first**, and write down each claim it makes and the number it uses.
2. **Read the evidence**: the [one-page summary](../../evidence/pilot-evidence-pack/README.md), then the four files of the pack. For each of Omar's claims, find the evidence for it and against it.
3. **Read the [evidence card](evidence-card.md)**: which numbers are recorded, which are assumed, and what is not known.
4. **Decide separately on each of the three changes**: go, no-go, or go with conditions.
5. **Write the memo** with the [template](../../templates/go-no-go-memo.md): the decision first, three reasons with their evidence and labels, the conditions, the pause conditions after launch, and what would change your mind.

## What a good memo does

- It decides each change on its own evidence, instead of one answer for the whole proposal.
- It uses the results on the kept-apart (holdout) tickets for the decision, and says how sure they are.
- It names the most expensive kind of error, and shows that the decision protects against it.
- It counts people's time, not only the model's price.
- It says which numbers are assumptions, and how to measure them.
- It sets conditions that someone can check, with an owner.

## Make it a portfolio piece

Publish your memo (one to two pages) with a short note: the decision you were asked to support, the evidence you used, and one thing you would measure next. Say that the company and its data are a course case study. A hiring manager for a product or operations role reads such a memo as proof that you can decide from evidence, not from a demo.
